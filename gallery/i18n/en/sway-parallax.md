## Intent
Forcing an ultra-wide image to shrink into the frame compresses every detail into an unreadable row of color blocks; cutting it into three split screens
breaks "this is one continuous line". The pan solves both at once: **preserving the clarity of the original size**
and **preserving the continuity of the horizontal sequence** — the audience literally sees step two growing to the right of step one. Vital points:
**speed has a ceiling** (the audience must skim-read while listening; beyond ~190px/s the text can't be followed),
**rotateY does only "following"** (≤4°; any more and it's no longer a pan but a 3D rotation, which belongs to [tilt-3d-page](tilt-3d-page.md)),
**no crash-stops at either end** (ease in at the start, settle extremely gently on arrival with nonzero terminal speed, keep drifting through the hold).

## Motion Core
- Three-layer structure: `.world` (`perspective: 1200px` — the pan uses a **large perspective**; too small a value makes the page's edges distort fisheye-style as it sweeps past) → `.camera` (camera layer, `preserve-3d`, 1880px wide ≈ twice the frame) → `.shadow` shadow layer + `.page` page layer
- The page must be **genuinely wider than the frame** (1.5~2×). This is the card's physical precondition: without width there is nothing to pan across
- Three-phase speed curve:
  1. **Pan ease-in** `sine.in` 0.55s — `sine.in`'s average speed is exactly half the terminal speed, so the distance covered in the ease-in = `panSpeed × accelDur / 2`, joining the uniform phase's speed **seamlessly** (skip this conversion and there's a speed step between the two phases, reading as "it stuttered")
  2. **Uniform pan** — `x` travels from start to end position; constant speed is the entirety of "the camera is panning"; the tail uses this library's shared camera-move ease `p + (1−r)·p²·(1−p)` (`r = endRate ≈ 0.5`) to settle extremely gently with nonzero terminal speed
  3. **Hold phase** — keeps drifting uniformly at the terminal speed `panSpeed × endRate`, with `rotateY` easing back a tiny bit (the camera never goes still)
- `rotateY` following: as the camera pans right (`x` going negative), the page's right side leans back 3~4°, on the same timeline as `x`. Its role is to make the "translation" read as **the camera walking past the page** rather than **an image sliding**; paired with a slight forward `z` shift (14px) to make the perspective displacement more legible
- Travel / speed / duration have only two degrees of freedom: `duration = travel ÷ speed`. In production, **fix the speed first** (the readability ceiling), then derive the duration from the material width, and finally check that the duration matches this narration segment; if it doesn't match, change the material width (crop a section) rather than speeding up
- The hold-phase endpoint must not exceed the material's right edge — `xTo + terminal speed × holdDur` must still lie within the page, or the hold will pan off the canvas and expose the background

## Parameters
| Parameter | Typical value | Tuning feel |
|------|--------|----------|
| `panSpeed` | 168px/s (960-wide frame; ceiling ~190) | Pan speed. >190px/s the text the audience is skimming gets yanked away, unfollowable; <90px/s drags the pace — the audience finishes reading the whole page ahead of the narration |
| `xFrom` / `xTo` | 40 / -770 px | Start and end positions. Start with the page's left edge visible in-frame **but leave a 40px margin** (flush against the edge exposes blank space beyond the material); the endpoint must leave headroom for the hold |
| `swayDeg` | 3.2° (ceiling 4°) | rotateY following amplitude. 0 = pure translation (reads as an image sliding — a notch weaker but safe); >6° it becomes a 3D-rotation card and the far-end body text starts being unreadable |
| `accelDur` | 0.55s | Pan ease-in. 0 hits full speed instantly, reading as a jump/seek; >1.2s the audience waits for it to get going |
| `endRate` | 0.5 | Terminal speed as a fraction of uniform speed. Settles without dead-stopping; 1 = uniform all the way (also valid, a bit more "machine") |
| `zLift` | 14px | The page's overall forward shift during the pan, making the perspective displacement more legible. 0 also works; >40px the page visibly enlarges, reading as a push-in |
| `perspective` (parent container) | 1200px | Large perspective dedicated to panning. <800px the page's edges distort fisheye-style during the sweep; >1600px the perspective is too weak and the rotateY following is wasted |
| `holdDur` | production 2~6s / demo 1.1s | Arrival dwell; this phase still drifts at terminal speed, and **the endpoint must not exceed the material's right edge** |

## Known Pitfalls
- Pan speed exceeding ~190px/s (normalized to a 960-wide frame) — the audience is reading text on the page; the words get yanked out of view, gone before a sentence is finished. This is the card's only hard ceiling; needing to go faster means this segment shouldn't be a pan (use a hard cut or whip-pan instead).
- Page only slightly wider than the frame (1.1~1.2×) — no travel to pan; a few dozen pixels and it's over, reading as "the frame twitched". If the width isn't there, showing the full page (with [slow-push-in](slow-push-in.md)) is more honest.
- Crash-stop at start/end (no ease-in, or `power2.out` bringing terminal speed to zero) — instant full speed reads as a programmatic seek; zero terminal speed dead-stops on the arrival frame. Both ends must stay continuous.
- `rotateY` pushed above 8° — no longer "the camera panning past the page" but the page rotating; the semantics become [tilt-3d-page](tilt-3d-page.md), and the far-end body text starts being unreadable.
- Ease-in distance not converted via `v × t / 2` — a speed step exists between the uniform and ease-in phases, which viewers read as "it stuttered at launch". This conversion is the technical essence of this card's timing.
- Hold phase panning past the material's right edge — the frame exposes the stage background, instantly fake. `xTo` must leave headroom for the hold.
- Pan direction against the reading direction (sweeping right-to-left across a left-to-right flow) — the audience's eyes and the camera fight each other. Horizontally set Chinese/English material always pans rightward (page moves left).
- Speed changes mid-pan (fast then slow) or overlaid hand-shake noise — the audience is skim-reading; speed noise directly induces motion sickness. This library does no stop-motion jitter.
- Misusing this card on a vertically long page — vertical scrolling is [evidence-scroll-tour](evidence-scroll-tour.md) (which carries the full grammar of "slow down and dwell at key points"); this card's horizontal pan is a different information structure.

## Reuse Guide
- HTML/GSAP: `demos/sway-parallax/index.html`. To change material, replace the DOM block inside `.page` (or swap in one ultra-wide `<img>`) and update `.camera`'s `width` to match; rhythm and posture all live in the top-level `CONFIG` (`panSpeed` / `xFrom` / `xTo` / `swayDeg` / `accelDur` / `holdDur` / `endRate` / `zLift`); the perspective value is `.world`'s `perspective`. **No manual duration math after changing `panSpeed` or travel** — the three phase durations are derived at runtime from `dist`/`accelDist`/`mainDur`. The extractable core: `CONFIG` + `camEase` + the `DemoShell.register` callback body.
- Remotion port: `x` is a three-segment `interpolate` — `interpolate(frame, [0, tAccel, tMain, tHold].map(s => s*fps), [xFrom, xFrom + dir*accelDist, xTo, xTo + dir*holdRate*holdDur])`, ease-in segment `Easing.in(Easing.sin)`, main segment `Easing.bezier(0.33,0.33,0.5,0.85)` (nonzero terminal speed), hold segment `Easing.linear`; segmented easing in Remotion either splits into three `interpolate` calls selected by `frame` range, or uses one `interpolate` with per-segment easing (v4's array-style easing requires manual segmentation). `rotateY` / `z` each get one line by the same method. Outer layer `perspective: 1200`, camera layer `transformStyle: 'preserve-3d'` + `transform: \`translateX(${x}px) translateZ(${z}px) rotateY(${ry}deg)\``. **The vertical variant** just swaps `x` for `y` and `rotateY` for `rotateX`; the speed ceiling is likewise set by readability.
- Editing-software equivalents: this is what everyone calls a **pan / horizontal camera move (the slow version of a whip pan)**. Jianying — two "position" keyframes on the ultra-wide image (X from +40 to -770), **set both ends linear** then add one easing keyframe at each end separately; "3D rotation" Y-axis keyed 0→3° as following. AE — keyframe Position's X, and in the Graph Editor set the start speed to 0 and the end speed to half the uniform speed (that's `endRate`); a 3D layer's `Y Rotation` follows at 3°; or invert it — fix the material and keyframe the Camera's Position (closer to the physics of "the camera moving"). Premiere — `Motion → Position` linear keyframes + a touch of `Basic 3D → Swivel` following.

## Scope
- Belongs to this card: one **horizontal pan speed curve** — `sine.in` ease-in 0.55s (ease-in distance converted via `v×t/2` to join the uniform phase seamlessly — the technical essence of this card's timing) → uniform sweep (speed ≤190px/s @960 width, the readability ceiling) → extremely gentle tail settle with **nonzero terminal speed** → hold drifting on at terminal speed; the `rotateY` ≤4° **following** tilt (on the same timeline as `x`, rewriting "an image sliding" into "the camera walking past the page") + slight forward `z` shift; the three-layer structure (parent `perspective` 800~1600px / camera layer `preserve-3d` / shadow layer); the two precondition disciplines "the page must be 1.5~2× wider than the frame" and "the pan must follow the reading direction"; the ordering rule `duration = travel ÷ speed` with **speed fixed first, duration derived after**.
- Does not belong to this card: the demo's grayscale wireframe wide-banner long page (brand area at the left / four flow cards and arrows in the middle / data summary grid at the right, with all their copy and figures), the specific travel and page-width values, the specific choice of panning "rightward" (set by the material's reading direction), the shadow density values, the material's entrance animation, annotations on the page.
- Migration interface: `panSpeed` is a quantity that **scales proportionally with frame width** (this card is designed at 168px/s for 960 width; a 1920 frame converts to ~336px/s — the readability ceiling scales with it); `xFrom/xTo` are computed from material width and frame width (40px margin at the start, hold headroom at the end); `swayDeg` caps the following amplitude at 4°; `accelDur` is the pan ease-in; `endRate` sets the tail; `perspective` scales proportionally with the frame. **Vertical variant** (panning up/down a long screenshot): `x→y`, `rotateY→rotateX`; all other parameters and disciplines unchanged.
- Background requirements: plain white is fine — this card has zero color, zero decoration; it is just one transform curve. Dark material works equally; only the shadow needs to be swapped for a bright edge, same as in [tilt-3d-page](tilt-3d-page.md).
