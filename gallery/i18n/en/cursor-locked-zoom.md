name: cursor-locked-zoom
title: A long command types out character by character in a grayscale terminal window while the camera, at 2.6~2.8x, stays hard-locked on the cursor (tx = W/2 − zoom·cursorX inverse solve + transform-origin 0 0); characters are "fed" in from the right edge of the frame with the cursor pinned dead center; after typing finishes, hold 0.75s for reading, then a 0.8s inOut pull of zoom back to 1 closes on a centered wide shot
usage: Reading out a long command, long path, long config, or long code line on a vertical/small screen — "this is the one you need to run"; also any segment where "the target is moving and the camera must follow" (a cursor, a progress dot, a form field being filled)
---

## Intent
Reading a long command on a small screen is a genuine dead zone for narration: lay a whole 60-character command across a vertical frame and the type is barely legible;
enlarge it enough to read and a single screen can't hold it. This card is the only solution to that dead zone — **don't make the viewer read the whole line; make them walk along with the person typing**.
The camera pushes in at 2.7x and locks onto the cursor; characters are "fed" in from the right while the cursor stays pinned at frame center:
at any moment the frame holds only the seven or eight characters currently being typed, and they're too large to miss.

What separates this beat from the library's other camera cards is **the camera's drive source**: other cards' cameras run on independent keyframes (time → pose),
while this card's camera **has no timeline of its own** — every frame it's computed from "how many characters have been revealed".
So when typing speeds up the camera speeds up, when typing pauses the camera pauses, and the two cannot desynchronize. If a port gives the camera its own displacement tween
to "chase" the typing, any rate change will pull them apart, and the look instantly collapses into "an enlarged terminal playing a typing animation".

The third constraint is the ending: **the follow shot must close on a wide shot**. The viewer has just walked the length of a command and has only fragments in their eyes;
they need one look at "what this command looks like as a whole, and in what context". Skipping this beat means the story wasn't finished.

## Motion Core
- Two structural layers: `.camera` (a world-coordinate layer filling the stage, `transform-origin: 0 0`, the card's only transformed element) → the asset (the terminal window). The asset's position in world coordinates is **fixed**; only the camera moves
- **`transform-origin: 0 0` is the precondition for the inverse solve**. The default `center` makes `scale` pivot around the element's center and the whole formula falls apart (the easiest line to miss in a port)
- **The core inverse solve (one line is all of it)**: `tx = W/2 − zoom·cursorX`, `ty = H/2 − zoom·cursorY`, `scale = zoom`. Meaning: "move the anchor's post-scale position to frame center"
- **The anchor is computed from the typing**: `cursorX = text start + revealed character count × char width`, `char width = fontSize × 0.6` (measured coefficient for monospace fonts like Menlo / SFMono).
  `cursorY = window top + chrome height + padding + row index × line height + line height/2` (line height = `round(fontSize × 1.6)`)
- **A monospace font is a hard precondition**: the whole anchor rides on "character count × char width"; with a proportional font every character differs in width and the inverse solve drifts further off with every keystroke
- **Boundary clamping (a required patch for the white stage; the original lacks it)**: cage the visible world rectangle inside the asset's four edges —
  `halfWidth = W/(2·zoom)`, `cx = clamp(cursorX, assetLeft + halfWidth, assetRight − halfWidth)`, likewise vertically; when the asset is smaller than the viewport it degrades to centering.
  The raw inverse solve pushes the frame outside the asset at line start/end; the original ran on a dark background so it never showed, but on a white stage it's a sweep of empty stage
- **The ending needs only one tweened value, `zoom`**: `zoom` shrinks ⇒ the visible rectangle grows ⇒ the clamp range narrows ⇒ the center is continuously squeezed back to the asset's middle; at `zoom = 1` the asset is smaller than the frame, the clamp degrades to centering ⇒ landing exactly on a centered wide shot. No second displacement tween needed (a freebie the clamping hands you)
- Typing-phase easing is `none` (linear): a human typing a command is steady; adding easing reads as "a machine speeding up and slowing down". Rate 15 char/s (a person keying a command — an order of magnitude slower than log output)
- Ending easing `power2.inOut` 0.8s: **both ends must settle** — this is an "ending" (an event with a beginning and an end), not an ongoing camera move
- Cursor state language: a 2px × 24px bar, **solid while typing, blinking at 2Hz only after stopping**. Solid = "outputting", blinking = "awaiting input" — that is the terminal's only state language; blinking throughout reads as "it froze"
- Asset context: a dark-based terminal window (the "command line" semantics live on the dark base) + three grayscale traffic lights; two dimmed lines of prior session history above (`ls` + its output) so the tracked line reads as "continuing to type after context", not a cold-open empty screen
- **The window must be wide enough that the whole command is legible at `zoom = 1`** — the ending delivers "the full picture"; if the command is still clipped by the window there, the beat was wasted

## Parameters
| Parameter | Typical value | Tuning feel |
|------|--------|----------|
| `zoom` | 2.65 (range 2.6~2.8) | The follow focal length. <2 fails at "legible even on small screens" (then why follow at all); >3.2 the type blows out soft and only four or five characters fit — the viewer loses all context |
| `rate` | 15 char/s (range 12~20) | Typing rate. >22 reads as "a machine pasting", not "a person typing"; <10 the viewer squirms waiting. **Commands must be an order of magnitude slower than log output** (logs run 60~70 char/s, see [terminal-typing-log](terminal-typing-log.md)) |
| `charW` | 0.6 | Monospace width coefficient. Menlo / SFMono / Consolas all measure 0.6; a font swap requires re-measuring (off by 0.02 and the cursor is half a character off by line end) |
| `fontSize` | 20px | Must match the CSS (char width and line height both derive from it). This is the `zoom = 1` size; the perceived size while following = 20 × 2.65 ≈ 53px |
| `lineH` | 32px (= round(fontSize × 1.6)) | Line height. For multi-line follows it's the vertical tracking step |
| `leadIn` | 0.45s | Opening hold. The camera locks on the line start waiting for the voice; 0 = typing starts before the viewer even registers it's a terminal |
| `holdEnd` | 0.75s (production 0.6~0.9s) | Post-typing hold. **Let people finish reading the command**; the cursor starts blinking here; <0.4s the viewer can't finish the last stretch |
| `pullDur` | 0.80s | Pull back to the wide shot. <0.5s reads as "snapping back"; >1.2s the viewer expects another destination |
| `cursorHz` | 2 | Blink frequency after stopping. The terminal-universal value; not recommended to change |
| Command length | 40~65 characters | <30 characters doesn't need a follow shot (one screen reads fine — give the wide shot straight); >80 the follow runs too long and viewers drift |

## Known Pitfalls
- **`transform-origin` left at the default `center`** — the inverse-solve formula breaks and every camera position is wrong (wrong in a "looks like a bug but you can't say where" way). Must be `0 0`.
- **Giving the camera its own displacement tween to "chase" the typing** — they desynchronize on any rate change. The camera must be recomputed every frame from `revealed character count`; that is this card's defining feature.
- **A proportional font** (default sans) — the "character count × char width" inverse solve drifts further per keystroke; by line end the cursor has left the frame. Monospace is a hard constraint.
- **Raw inverse solve without boundary clamping** — at the first few characters and at completion, the frame gets pushed outside the asset. The original (remocn) had a dark background so it never showed; on a white stage it's a sweep of blank. This is the segment a port to this library must add.
- Easing on the typing phase (`power2` and the like) — reads as "a machine speeding up and slowing down"; human keystrokes are steady. This segment's ease must be `none`.
- Cursor blinking the whole way through — reads as "the terminal froze / is awaiting input", fighting the "currently outputting" semantics. **Solid while typing, blink only after stopping.**
- Ending without the wide shot — the viewer holds only fragments, never seeing the command whole. The follow must close on the full picture.
- Window too narrow, command still clipped at `zoom = 1` — the closing beat wasted. Back-solve the window width from "longest command × char width + padding + prompt".
- `zoom` above 3.5 — only four or five characters per screen; the viewer loses context entirely and the follow becomes "swimming in a soup of letters".
- Using it on a short command (`npm i`) — unnecessary. Under 30 characters, a straight wide shot + one [slow-push-in](slow-push-in.md) is enough; this card's cost doesn't pay off.
- Following **two** moving targets in one shot (cursor + progress bar) — the camera can lock only one. To tell two, split into two beats.

## Reuse Guide
- HTML/GSAP: `demos/cursor-locked-zoom/index.html`. Change the command via `CONFIG.cmd` (and re-check window width against the new length); rate and focal length in `CONFIG.rate` / `CONFIG.zoom`; when swapping the asset for a different window, edit the `CONFIG.winL/winT/winW/winH/pad/chromeH` geometry group (used both to derive the anchor and as the camera's clamp boundary). Liftable core: `CONFIG` + `apply()` (the seven-or-eight lines of inverse solve + clamping).
- Minimal asset-swap flow: ① fill the asset's four world-coordinate edges into `winL/winT/winW/winH`; ② replace the anchor formula with "the center coordinates of the moving target inside the asset" (not necessarily a text cursor — a progress dot, a form field being filled, or the line being highlighted all work); ③ touch nothing else. **As long as you can write the one function "the target center's world coordinates", this card can follow it.**
- **Multi-line code follow** (the `glass-code-walk` variant at the source): change `cmdRow` from an integer to a floating-point row number, `cursorY = window top + chrome + padding + linePosition × lineH + lineH/2`, with `linePosition` interpolating between adjacent rows (scanning from line 3 to line 4 it runs 3.0 → 4.0) ⇒ the camera glides between lines instead of snapping. Horizontally you can lock x to the line start only (code is read by whole lines, not character by character), or take each line's midpoint.
- Remotion port (this is the source): `registry/remocn/terminal-cursor-zoom/index.tsx` (143 lines, the shortest and cleanest). It derives `revealed` via `interpolate(localFrame, [0, len/charsPerFrame], [0, len])`, then the same inverse solve; the `chunkSize` parameter does chunked bursts (this card's command line uses 1 = per-character; only logs use 4, see [terminal-typing-log](terminal-typing-log.md)). Add the boundary clamping when porting.
- Editing-software equivalents: AE — drop the terminal screenshot/recording into a comp, add a Null as the "cursor anchor", keyframe its position to the typing rhythm, then bind the camera's or layer's position with an **expression** `W/2 − zoom × null.position[0]` (an expression is far more accurate than hand keyframes, and the only way to guarantee no desync); the zoom pull-back is two `Scale` keyframes + Easy Ease on both sides. JianYing — manual approximation only: enlarge the recording and lay a string of "position" keyframes to the typing rhythm (it will desync, and long commands mean huge keyframe counts) — not recommended. Premiere — same via `Motion`'s Position keyframes, or dynamic-link to After Effects. **In real production the commoner path is a raw screen recording + tracked pan in post**, but a recording's typing rate is uncontrollable (human hands are uneven); this card's value is precisely that both rate and camera are directable.
- Sound: `typekey` in sparse bursts — **do not sound every character** (50 characters means 50 hits smearing into noise). Group by "word": 2~3 hits at each word's start, `vol` alternating within 0.24~0.30, `rate` alternating within 0.94~1.24 (a repeated sample must vary pitch or it reads as a mechanical loop); one `whoosh` on the pull to wide (`vol` 0.26, `rate` 0.92). The hold is silent.

## Scope
- Belongs to this card: the whole **camera-locked-on-a-moving-target follow** — the inverse-solve formula `tx = W/2 − zoom·cursorX` (with `transform-origin: 0 0` as precondition), the defining discipline that **the camera is driven by content progress, not independent keyframes**, the anchor computed from "revealed characters × char width (fontSize × 0.6)", the monospace hard constraint, the boundary clamping that cages the visible rectangle inside the asset's edges (plus the freebie of "tween only zoom; displacement follows from the clamp"), the `ease: none` steady-rate discipline of the typing phase, the 2.6~2.8x focal band, the "solid cursor while typing / 2Hz blink after stopping" state language, and the closing beat of a 0.8s `power2.inOut` pull to `zoom = 1` centered wide (including the "window width must make the full picture legible" back-solve discipline).
- Does not belong to this card: the demo's terminal-window visuals (dark base / three traffic lights / path title / two lines of session history above), the ffmpeg command itself and its content, the command-line subject matter (the tracked target can be a progress dot, a form field, a highlighted line), the specific 15 char/s rate, the specific 760×384 asset geometry, and the terminal palette.
- Migration interfaces: filling the asset's four edges into `winL/winT/winW/winH` completes the clamp setup; writing "the target center's world coordinates" as a progress-evaluated function completes the follow setup (these two are the only things this card needs adapted); validate `zoom` by "is the smallest type visually legible while following" (not by composition) — at 1080 wide vertical, `fontSize × zoom ≥ 44px` is the safety line; set `rate` to the narration pace (he names a parameter, you type a parameter — don't let the camera outrun the explanation); `charW` must be re-measured per font; back-solve window width from the longest command.
- Background requirements: a white stage works (demo-verified), but **the asset itself needs a dark base** — the "command line" semantics live on it. If the tracked subject isn't a terminal (say a light form UI), a light asset works too, but then the boundary clamping is even less optional (a light asset against a white stage has no luminance separation; an exposed edge reads as "the asset vanished").
- Boundaries with the library's other camera cards: [slow-push-in](slow-push-in.md) / [slow-pull-reveal](slow-pull-reveal.md) have static targets and cameras on independent keyframes; [stage-keyframe-tour](stage-keyframe-tour.md) is multi-keyframe docking at **preset** points of interest (positions known before the animation), while this card's target position **is only known at runtime** (it depends on which character has been typed) — that is the dividing line between "keyframed camera" and "follow shot". On the asset side: [terminal-typing-log](terminal-typing-log.md) covers a terminal **advancing line by line + scrolling** (camera still, content moving), while this card is one long line + a moving camera; the two cards chain well (follow the command as it's read, pull to wide, then cut to the scrolling log).
